# Product lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **Crew runs could be the one thing.** Members in one room start together, run as a group, and share a result. Korean running crews are a real social habit, and this is a moment no solo tracker gives you. It is the only feature in the brief that could not be swapped for Strava plus a group chat.
- **The thesis puts the product first.** "The app should be worth opening without the token", a v1 that is points only, and earnings capped by design all mean the founder wants running to be the reward, not a payout.
- **Care in the details nobody sees.** It has a lock-screen live notification, GPS checks, a speed-limit stop with a penalty, and daily caps. Anti-cheat and the lock screen are unglamorous work, and someone did them.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No focus. It is four products in one Android app.** It is a run tracker, a crew social network (chat, invites, flash meetups, leaderboards), a sneaker-collecting economy (6 rarities, durability, repair, levelling, trading), and a hidden blockchain layer. AI coaching screens also ship switched off. A solo founder cannot make four things brilliant. Right now none of them is the clear reason to open the app.
2. **It cannot be explained in one sentence.** To understand a crew run, a new user has to learn shoe durability, crew stamina (max durability × crew level), 5 stamina per minute, one crew run a day, rewards that stop at 0 while recording continues, and a headcount bonus. That bonus needs 500 m distance at the start and at 2 minutes, plus phone verification. If the founder needs a paragraph, the user is lost in the first five minutes.
3. **Seams the user will feel.** The headcount bonus is designed into the experience but pays 0 today, because nobody has phone verification. The store build hides wallets and NFTs, so the game economy points to a payoff that the shipped app never delivers. SUP has "no cash value", yet the app asks you to grind, repair and trade for it. Each of these is a moment where the promise and the reality come apart.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence, what is StepUp for? Who opens it, and why, on a Tuesday?
- Walk me through the first five minutes after install for a runner with no crew. What do they see, what do they tap, and when do they feel something good?
- If you had to delete half the features before launch, which half goes? Is the sneaker economy the product or decoration?
- Why ship features that show zero (the headcount bonus) or are switched off (AI coaching)?
- What does a crew leader experience when setting up a crew run for 8 people? Where does it break: invites, the waiting room, GPS, or the 2-minute check?
- No user metrics exist yet. What single number would tell you the core moment works?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Pick the one thing and cut to it.** My read is that it is crew runs. Launch v1 as a tracker plus crews plus crew runs. Hide or remove anything that does not serve that moment: AI coaching, trading, and probably most of the shoe levelling and repair loop.
2. **A one-sentence pitch a stranger repeats back** before the store listing is written.
3. **Remove every dead promise from the shipped build.** That means no bonus that pays 0, no screens that are switched off, and no economy that hints at a cash-out the app cannot give.
4. **Watch 10 real first sessions and one real crew run end to end**, then fix the seams found there before doing any acquisition. Report how many first-session users finish a run and how many come back for a second crew run.
