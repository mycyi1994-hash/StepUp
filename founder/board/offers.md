# Offers lens
Score: 3 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- **The crew is the real offer, not the token.** Running together with your crew, starting in one room, sharing one stamina pool and a crew level, is a concrete dream outcome ("my crew runs more, together"). The game makes showing up a group thing, not a solo chore.
- **Time to first result is near zero.** GPS route, pace, laps, a shareable result card and a leaderboard on run one. On the value equation, time is the one term this product already wins.
- **The founder already refuses to compete on token yield.** Capped earnings and "app first, token second" mean the pitch is not "earn more than elsewhere", which this lens would treat as a commodity race to collapse.
- **The stack is mostly built and cheap to run.** Courses, crew chat, flash runs and a weekly board are low-cost add-ons that each solve a real step on the way to "a crew that keeps running".

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **There is no buyer and no offer.** Free, no ads, no subscription, no purchases, no revenue model. This lens scores an offer someone pays for, and none exists. Nobody has said who has the pain *and the money*. Is it runners, crew leaders, running clubs, brands or gyms? Until that is named, "the market" is just "Korean runners who might download a free app", and nobody has sized it.
2. **The effort term is very heavy and the likelihood term has no proof.** To get the full reward a runner has to understand shoes, rarities, durability, repair, levelling, a daily crew stamina pool, one crew run per day, staying within 500 m at the start and 2 minutes in, and phone verification. The headcount bonus is 0 for everyone today. No user metrics exist. Lots of effort, no proof, and part of the promised outcome is switched off. That is the profile of an offer people say no to.
3. **The dream outcome got hollowed out by the store build.** "Earn while you run" is the hook a game layer implies, but in the Play build SUP has no cash value and cannot be withdrawn. Without the token, the reward loop is points you spend on keeping virtual shoes alive. The founder's thesis is that the app is worth opening without the token, and that is untested. If it is false, nothing is left to stack on.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Who pays, eventually, and for what outcome? Name one buyer and one price, even a hypothetical one.
- For a crew leader, what is the painful problem today (getting people to show up? keeping a club together?), and how do they solve it now?
- What proof will make a runner believe this makes their crew run more? Which metric, and over how many weeks?
- What can you cut from the game layer so a new runner gets the whole reward in their first crew run?
- What would a guarantee look like here? For example: "your crew runs X more times a month or …".
- How do the first 100 users arrive, and does any of them already pay for something running-related?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Not applicable, because the vote is PASS. What would move it to FUND IF:
1. A named paying buyer (for example crew or club leaders, or a sponsor) with a stated offer and price. The "free forever, decide later" approach does not count.
2. Retention evidence from real crews using the off-chain build, which tests the "worth opening without the token" claim.
3. A simpler first-run path where the headcount bonus actually works, either by enabling phone verification or by dropping that requirement, so the offer delivers the outcome it advertises.
