## Risks raised by more than one member
1. **No buyer, no revenue model** (Offers, Monopoly): free, no ads, no purchases; nobody named who pays or what for.
2. **The game layer costs effort and promises a payoff the store build never delivers** (all three): shoes, durability, repair, crew stamina, 500 m / 2-minute checks; SUP has no cash value; the headcount bonus pays 0 because nobody has phone verification.
3. **Crew runs are the only real edge, and they are not proven** (all three): no users, no retention data; Android-only splits real crews.
4. **No distribution plan** (Monopoly, Offers): how the first 100 users or first crews arrive, and at what cost, is unknown.

## The strongest version the board can see
A Korean **crew-running tool**, not a move-to-earn game: the crew leader sets up a weekly run, members start together in one room, the run is recorded, the crew's shared progress and level grow, and the result is shared back to the crew chat. The shoe economy becomes optional decoration or goes. That differs from the pitch — the pitch leads with points and sneakers; the board would lead with "get your crew to show up and run together". Disagreement kept: Product would fund that narrowed version on conditions; Offers and Monopoly pass until someone pays and real crews retain.
